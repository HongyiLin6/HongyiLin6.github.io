Hongyi Lin (林泓熠)

hy-lin22@mails.tsinghua.edu.cn · CV · Google Scholar

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at the School of Vehicle and Mobility, Tsinghua University, supervised by Prof. Xiaobo Qu, and expect to graduate in June 2027. From August 2025 to June 2026, I was a visiting Ph.D. student at MIT, where I worked with Prof. Jinhua Zhao and Prof. Haris Koutsopoulos.

I bring AI and generative modeling techniques to transportation and autonomous systems, with my research broadly spanning four interconnected themes. (1) Autonomous Driving focuses on multimodal and cooperative perception, as well as the controllable generation of diverse open-world traffic scenarios. (2) World Models / Physical AI develops physics-informed generative models for construction machinery, low-altitude aerial autonomy in urban environments, and other embodied systems. (3) Intelligent Transportation Systems studies demand prediction, traffic-state modeling, multi-agent control, and large-scale transportation data analytics. (4) Generative AI investigates how LLMs, VLMs, and VLAs can support perception, reasoning, model collaboration, and decision-making in transportation and autonomous systems.

I have also conducted applied research at the Intelligent Connected Vehicle Center of the Tsinghua University Suzhou Automotive Research Institute, SAIC Z-One Tech, and Tsing-AI (Shanghai).

Ongoing Research

PileBelief: Persistent Physical State for Interaction-Driven World Modeling

PileBelief is an interaction-driven world model for excavation that retains physical evidence beyond the visible surface. It combines an observation-conditioned prior with persistent spatial and physical-response memories built from completed interactions. Action-aligned reads and gated corrections refine predictions of terrain change, payload, force, material flow, and failure. Measured interactions update physical belief, while hypothetical actions evolve a separate imagined state with fixed model weights. This supports multi-step prediction and candidate-action ranking under partial observation, with validation in simulation and real excavation. Manuscript under review.

World Models / Physical AI
WindCity: A Physics-Informed World Model for Urban Low-Altitude Autonomy

Existing UAV world models rarely account for realistic wind effects, while conventional wind-field generation relies heavily on computationally intensive numerical solvers or commercial software, limiting real-time synthesis. We propose a physics-informed UAV world model based on a Diffusion-MoE architecture that jointly generates 3D urban layouts, wind fields, and future scene evolution to support low-altitude prediction, planning, and safety. Manuscript in preparation.
